One y-axis, always. A dual-axis chart invites a reader to compare two scales that were never comparable. Two measures of different magnitude want two charts, or an index to a common base.

Bars sit on zero. A magnitude axis that starts anywhere else makes a 3% gap look like 300%. Lines may start above zero, because a trend is about change.

Show table is not a nicety. Three of the eight series colours sit below 3:1 against a white surface — legal only with relief, and a real table is the relief. It also makes the figures copyable, which is what anyone assembling a deliverable actually wants.
